Choosing the right lot size is the single most important risk-management skill in trading. With the correct position size, a losing trade costs only a small, planned part of your account — no matter how wide your stop loss is.

## Lot sizes explained

| Lot | Units | Pip value on EUR/USD |
|---|---|---|
| Standard lot (1.00) | 100,000 | about $10 per pip |
| Mini lot (0.10) | 10,000 | about $1 per pip |
| Micro lot (0.01) | 1,000 | about $0.10 per pip |

For pairs where the US dollar is the second currency (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, AUD/USD), one standard lot is worth about **$10 per pip**. For other pairs the pip value changes with the exchange rate — your platform shows the exact value.

## The lot size formula

> **Lot size = Risk amount ÷ (Stop loss in pips × Pip value per lot)**

Where:
- **Risk amount** = account balance × risk % (for example 1%)
- **Stop loss in pips** = the distance from your entry to your stop
- **Pip value per lot** = about $10 for USD-quoted pairs

## Example 1 – EUR/USD

- Account: **$1,000**, risk **1%** → risk amount **$10**
- Stop loss: **20 pips**
- Lot size = 10 ÷ (20 × 10) = **0.05 lots**

If the stop is hit, you lose about $10 — exactly 1% of your account.

## Example 2 – Gold (XAU/USD)

On most brokers **1 lot of gold = 100 ounces**, so a $1.00 move in the gold price equals **$100 per lot**.

- Account: **$2,000**, risk **1%** → **$20**
- Stop loss: **$5.00** below entry
- Lot size = 20 ÷ (5 × 100) = **0.04 lots**

Learn more in our guide on [how to trade gold](/guides/how-to-trade-gold-xauusd/).

## Example 3 – USD/JPY

For JPY pairs a pip is 0.01 and the pip value depends on the USD/JPY rate (roughly $6–7 per pip per standard lot at recent prices). Use your platform's contract information or a calculator, then apply the same formula.

## Indices and crypto

For NAS100, US30 or crypto CFDs, check the **contract size** in your broker's specification (for example $1 per point per lot). The formula stays the same: risk ÷ (stop distance × value per point). See [how to trade NAS100 and US30](/guides/how-to-trade-nas100-and-us30/).

## Common mistakes

- **Using the same lot size for every trade** even when the stop distance changes.
- **Risking 5–10% per trade** — a short losing streak can empty the account.
- **Moving the stop loss further away** after entering.
- **Forgetting the spread**, which adds a little to every stop.

## Frequently asked questions

### What lot size should a beginner use with $100?

With a 1% risk ($1) and a 20-pip stop, the correct size is 0.005 lots — below the 0.01 minimum. A cent account (offered by brokers like [PU Prime](/brokers/pu-prime/) and [Exness](/brokers/exness/)) lets you trade such small sizes.

### Is 0.01 lot safe?

It depends on your stop loss and balance. Always calculate rather than guess.

### Does leverage change the lot size calculation?

No. Leverage only changes the margin you need; your risk depends on lot size and stop distance.
