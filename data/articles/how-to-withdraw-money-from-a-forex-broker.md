Getting your money out quickly is just as important as depositing it. Most delays come from missing documents or using a different method than the deposit. Follow these steps for smooth, fast withdrawals.

## Before your first withdrawal

1. **Verify your account fully** – upload your ID and proof of address. Unverified accounts can't withdraw.
2. **Use payment methods in your own name** – third-party payments are rejected by regulated brokers.
3. **Check free margin** – you can only withdraw funds not used as margin for open trades.

## The "return to source" rule

Regulated brokers follow anti-money-laundering rules. Withdrawals usually go back to the **same method you deposited with**, up to the deposited amount. Profits above that can then be sent to another method in your name, such as a bank account. This rule protects you as well as the broker.

## Step-by-step withdrawal

1. Log in to your broker's **client area** (website or app).
2. Click **Withdraw**.
3. Choose the **trading account** and the **method**.
4. Enter the **amount** and your payment details (for crypto: the wallet address and network).
5. Confirm with the code sent to your email or phone.
6. Track the request status in your client area.

## Tips for fast withdrawals

- **Verify before you deposit**, not when you want to withdraw.
- **Use one method** for deposits and withdrawals whenever possible.
- **Withdraw during business days** for bank and card methods.
- **Keep your contact details up to date** so confirmations arrive.
- **Save transaction IDs** until the money arrives.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a forex withdrawal take?

E-wallet and crypto withdrawals are often processed within hours; bank and card withdrawals can take a few business days.

### Are there withdrawal fees?

Many brokers don't charge their own withdrawal fee on most methods, but banks, card issuers or blockchain networks may.

### Why was my withdrawal rejected?

The most common reasons are unverified accounts, payment details in another person's name, or not enough free margin.
