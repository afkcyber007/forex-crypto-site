Trading signals are trade ideas shared by another trader, usually through Telegram. Used well, they help you learn and find setups. Used blindly, they are a quick way to lose money. Here is how to use them properly.

## What a signal contains

A complete signal gives you:

- **Instrument and direction** — for example BUY XAUUSD or SHORT BTCUSDT.
- **Entry price or zone** — where to open the trade.
- **Stop loss** — where the idea is proven wrong.
- **Take-profit targets** — TP1, TP2 and so on.

If a signal has no stop loss, do not trade it.

## Size the position yourself

The signal provider does not know your balance. Work out your own position size so that hitting the stop loss costs no more than 1–2% of your account. Our [risk management guide](/guides/risk-management-for-traders/) shows the formula.

## Managing the trade

- Close part of the position at TP1 and move your stop to break-even if the provider suggests it.
- Do not chase: if price is already far past the entry zone, skip the trade.
- Avoid entering just before major news releases.

## Red flags of fake signal providers

- Promises of guaranteed profits or "99% accuracy".
- Results shown only as screenshots of winning trades, with no full history.
- Pressure to deposit with an unknown broker or send money to a "fund manager".
- Requests for your trading account password or exchange API keys with withdrawal permission.
- Deleting losing signals from the channel.

A trustworthy provider shows losses as well as wins, explains the reasoning, and never asks for your login details.
