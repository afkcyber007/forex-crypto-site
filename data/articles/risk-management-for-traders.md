Good risk management will not make a bad strategy profitable, but bad risk management will ruin a good one. These are the rules that keep traders in the game.

## The 1% rule

Risk no more than 1% (at most 2%) of your account on a single trade. With a $1,000 account, a losing trade should cost you about $10. Ten losses in a row — which happens to every trader — would then cost about 10%, not your whole account.

## Always use a stop loss

A stop loss is an order that closes your trade automatically at a set price. Decide where it goes **before** you enter, based on the chart (beyond a swing high or low), not on how much you hope to lose. Never move a stop further away once the trade is open.

## Position size from your stop

Position size = Amount at risk ÷ (Stop distance × Value per pip or point).

Example: you risk $10, your stop is 20 pips away on EURUSD, and 0.01 lot is worth about $0.10 per pip. 20 pips × $0.10 = $2 per 0.01 lot, so you can trade 0.05 lots.

## Risk–reward ratio

If you risk $10 to make $20, your risk–reward is 1:2. With 1:2 trades you can be right only 40% of the time and still make money before costs. Avoid trades where the potential reward is smaller than the risk.

## Set daily and weekly loss limits

Professional traders stop for the day after losing a set amount (for example 3% of the account) and review after a bad week. This prevents "revenge trading" — trying to win losses back quickly with bigger positions.

## Keep a trading journal

Record every trade: the reason for entering, entry, stop, target, result and how you felt. After 50–100 trades the journal shows what really works for you.

## Extra rules for crypto futures

- Prefer isolated margin and low leverage.
- Watch funding rates on longer holds.
- Do not keep all your capital on one exchange; withdraw profits to your own wallet.
