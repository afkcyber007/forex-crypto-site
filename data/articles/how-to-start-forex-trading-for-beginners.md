Forex is the world's biggest financial market, open 24 hours a day from Monday to Friday. Anyone with a phone and a small deposit can start, but beginners who follow a clear plan last much longer than those who jump straight in. This guide takes you from zero to your first trade, step by step.

## Step 1 – Understand what you are trading

In forex you always trade one currency against another, such as **EUR/USD** or **GBP/JPY**. If you think the euro will rise against the dollar, you **buy** EUR/USD; if you think it will fall, you **sell**. Most beginners also trade gold (XAU/USD) and indices like NAS100 and US30 through the same platform.

New to the basics? Read [what is forex trading](/guides/what-is-forex-trading/) first.

## Step 2 – Choose a regulated broker

Your broker holds your money and executes your trades, so this is the most important decision. Look for:

- **Regulation** by a well-known authority (FCA, ASIC, CySEC, FSCA and others)
- **Low minimum deposit**, so you can start small
- **MT4 or MT5** platforms and a good mobile app
- **Fast deposits and withdrawals** in your country

## Step 3 – Open a demo account first

Every good broker offers a free **demo account** with virtual money. Use it for at least two to four weeks to learn the platform, practise placing orders and test your strategy without risking real money. Our guide on [how to open a demo trading account](/guides/how-to-open-a-demo-trading-account/) shows you how.

## Step 4 – Learn the key terms

- **Pip** – the smallest standard price move (0.0001 on most pairs).
- **Lot** – the trade size. 1 standard lot = 100,000 units; 0.01 is a micro lot.
- **Leverage** – lets you control a larger position with less money. It increases both profits and losses — read [forex leverage explained](/guides/forex-leverage-explained/).
- **Spread** – the small difference between the buy and sell price; this is the main trading cost.
- **Stop loss / take profit** – orders that close your trade automatically at a loss limit or profit target.

## Step 5 – Build a simple plan

Write down three things before every trade:

1. **Why** you are entering (your setup).
2. **Where** you are wrong (your stop loss).
3. **How much** you are risking — most professionals risk only **1–2% of their account per trade**.

Use our guide to [calculate lot size](/guides/how-to-calculate-lot-size-in-forex/) so every trade risks the same amount.

## Step 6 – Place your first live trade

When you are consistent on demo, open a live account and start small:

1. Deposit an amount you can afford to lose.
2. Open the pair you practised on.
3. Choose a small lot size (for example 0.01).
4. Set your [stop loss and take profit](/guides/how-to-set-stop-loss-and-take-profit/) before you click Buy or Sell.
5. Record the trade in a journal.

## Step 7 – Keep learning and stay consistent

Follow the [economic calendar](/guides/how-to-read-the-economic-calendar/), learn to [read candlestick charts](/guides/how-to-read-candlestick-charts/), and review your journal every week. Free [forex signals](/free-forex-signals/) can help you see how experienced traders plan entries, stops and targets.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much money do I need to start forex trading?

Some brokers accept as little as $10, but $100–$500 gives you more room to manage risk properly.

### Can I learn forex trading on my own?

Yes. With a demo account, free guides and a trading journal, many traders teach themselves. Patience matters more than talent.

### How long does it take to become profitable?

It varies widely. Most traders need months of practice; focus on following your plan and protecting your capital first.

*Trading forex and CFDs with leverage carries a high risk of losing money. Only trade money you can afford to lose.*
