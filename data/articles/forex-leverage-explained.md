Leverage is the feature that makes forex trading accessible with small deposits — and it is also the main reason most retail traders lose money. Here is how it works.

## What is leverage?

Leverage lets you control a large position with a small amount of your own money. With leverage of 1:100, every $1 in your account controls $100 in the market. With 1:500, $1 controls $500.

## What is margin?

Margin is the money the broker sets aside from your balance to keep a position open. The formula is simple:

- **Margin = Position size ÷ Leverage**

For example, buying 1 standard lot of EURUSD (100,000 euros, about $108,000) with 1:100 leverage needs about $1,080 of margin. With 1:500 leverage it needs about $216.

## Leverage does not change your profit per pip

This is the most misunderstood point. On EURUSD one standard lot is worth about $10 per pip whatever leverage you use. Leverage only changes how much margin is locked, and therefore **how big a position you are allowed to open**. Danger comes from opening positions that are too large for your balance.

## Margin call and stop-out

- **Margin level** = Equity ÷ Used margin × 100%.
- When the margin level falls to the broker's **margin call** level (for example 60% on Exness Standard), you are warned.
- At the **stop-out** level the broker starts closing your positions automatically to stop your balance going negative.

## Leverage limits around the world

Regulators cap leverage for retail clients: 1:30 on major pairs in the UK, EU and Australia, and 1:25 in Japan. Offshore entities often allow 1:500, 1:1000 or more. Higher limits are not a reason to use them.

## How much leverage should you use?

A safer approach is to ignore the maximum and size every trade by risk:

1. Decide to risk 1% of your balance (for example $5 on a $500 account).
2. Set your stop loss at a logical level, for example 25 pips away.
3. Position size = $5 ÷ (25 pips × pip value). On EURUSD that is 0.02 lots.

If you size positions this way, the leverage setting becomes a safety buffer instead of a trap.

## Gold (XAUUSD) moves faster

Gold often moves $20–$40 in a day. One standard lot of XAUUSD (100 ounces) changes by $100 for every $1 move in price, so position sizes for gold should be much smaller than for EURUSD.
