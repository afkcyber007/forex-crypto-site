Most crypto losses are not caused by exchange hacks — they come from phishing, weak passwords and scams. These ten steps make your account much harder to attack.

## 1. Use a unique, strong password

Use a password manager to create a long, random password that you use nowhere else.

## 2. Turn on app-based 2FA

Use an authenticator app (such as Google Authenticator) or a hardware key instead of SMS codes, which can be stolen through SIM-swap attacks. Back up your 2FA recovery key offline.

## 3. Set an anti-phishing code

Exchanges such as Bitunix and MEXC let you set a personal code that appears in every genuine email. If an email does not show your code, it is fake.

## 4. Enable a withdrawal address whitelist

With a whitelist, funds can only be withdrawn to addresses you approved in advance — often with a 24-hour delay for new addresses.

## 5. Bookmark the official website

Always open the exchange from a saved bookmark or the official app. Fake sites copy logos exactly and are often promoted through ads and Telegram messages.

## 6. Never share API keys with withdrawal permission

If you connect a trading bot, create API keys with **trading only** permission and restrict them to the bot's IP address.

## 7. Ignore "support" in private messages

Real exchange support will never message you first on Telegram or WhatsApp or ask for your password, 2FA code or seed phrase.

## 8. Complete KYC early

Verifying your identity before you need to withdraw avoids your funds being locked at a stressful moment.

## 9. Keep long-term savings in your own wallet

Keep only active trading capital on an exchange. Store long-term holdings in a hardware wallet whose seed phrase only you control.

## 10. Do not use a VPN to get around restrictions

Using a VPN to access an exchange that does not serve your country breaks its terms and can lead to frozen withdrawals. Check our [country guides](/countries/) to find exchanges that accept you.
