Shorting lets you profit when a cryptocurrency's price **falls**. It is a key tool for active traders and for hedging long-term holdings. Here is how it works and how to do it safely.

## What does "shorting" mean?

When you **short** (sell first), you open a position that gains value as the price drops and loses value as the price rises. On crypto exchanges this is done with **perpetual futures** — contracts that track the coin's price without an expiry date.

**Example:** you short 0.01 BTC at $100,000. If BTC falls to $95,000, the position gains $50 (0.01 × $5,000). If BTC rises to $105,000, it loses $50.

## Step-by-step: how to short on a crypto exchange

1. **Open an account** on a futures exchange such as [Bitunix](/exchanges/bitunix/), [WEEX](/exchanges/weex/), [BloFin](/exchanges/blofin/) or [Bybit](/exchanges/bybit/).
2. **Transfer USDT** to your futures (or unified) account.
3. **Choose the pair**, for example BTCUSDT perpetual.
4. **Select margin mode** – **isolated** margin limits the risk to that position; beginners usually prefer it.
5. **Set low leverage** – 2x to 5x is a sensible start.
6. **Enter the size** and attach a **stop loss above your entry** and a take profit below.
7. Click **Sell / Short**.
8. To close, click **Close** or let your TP/SL execute.

## Funding rates

Perpetual futures use a **funding rate** to keep their price close to the spot price. It is usually paid every 8 hours on most pairs (some pairs settle more often). When funding is **positive**, longs pay shorts; when it is **negative**, shorts pay longs. Check the funding rate before holding a short for several days.

## Risks to manage

- **Prices can rise sharply**, especially during short squeezes.
- **Leverage magnifies losses** — high leverage can lead to [liquidation](/guides/how-to-avoid-liquidation-in-crypto-futures/) with a small move.
- **Always use a stop loss** — see [how to set stop loss and take profit](/guides/how-to-set-stop-loss-and-take-profit/).
- **Size each trade** so a stop-out costs only 1–2% of your account.

## When do traders short?

- In a clear **downtrend** (lower highs and lower lows).
- After a **rejection** from strong resistance.
- To **hedge** spot holdings during a correction without selling them.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I short crypto without futures?

Some platforms offer margin trading, but perpetual futures are the most common and accessible way.

### Is shorting riskier than buying?

When you buy, the most you can lose is what you put in. With a leveraged short, losses can grow quickly if price rises, which is why stop losses and isolated margin matter.

### Which coins can I short?

Major exchanges list hundreds of perpetual pairs, from BTC and ETH to many altcoins.
