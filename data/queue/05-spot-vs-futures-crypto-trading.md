Every crypto exchange offers two main ways to trade: **spot** and **futures**. They look similar on screen but work very differently. Choosing the right one is one of the most important decisions for a beginner.

## What is spot trading?

In spot trading you **buy the actual coin**. If you buy 0.01 BTC, you own 0.01 BTC. You can hold it, send it to a wallet or sell it later.

- You profit only if the price **rises**.
- There is **no leverage**, so you can never lose more than you invested.
- There are **no funding fees**, so you can hold for years.

## What is futures trading?

In futures trading you trade a **contract** that follows the price — you do not own the coin. Most exchanges offer **perpetual futures**, which never expire.

- You can go **long** (profit if price rises) or **short** (profit if price falls).
- You can use **leverage** — 5x, 20x, even 100x or more.
- If the price moves too far against you, your position is **liquidated**.
- Holding positions costs or pays **funding fees** every few hours.

## Spot vs futures at a glance

| | Spot | Futures |
|---|---|---|
| Own the coin | Yes | No |
| Profit when price falls | No | Yes (short) |
| Leverage | No | Yes |
| Liquidation risk | No | Yes |
| Funding fees | No | Yes |
| Best for | Long-term holding, beginners | Active traders with risk rules |

## Which should beginners choose?

Start with **spot**. It is simpler, and a bad trade cannot liquidate your account. When you move to futures:

1. Use **isolated margin**.
2. Keep leverage at **2x–5x** while learning.
3. Always set a **stop loss** well before the liquidation price.
4. Risk no more than **1–2%** of your balance per trade.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is futures trading riskier than spot?
Yes. Leverage magnifies both gains and losses, and positions can be liquidated.

### Can I lose more than I deposit on futures?
On most major exchanges, losses are limited to the margin in your position or account — but you can still lose all of it quickly.

### What are funding fees?
Small payments between long and short traders every few hours that keep the futures price close to the spot price.
