A **funded trading account** lets you trade a large account — for example $10,000 to $200,000 — and keep most of the profits, without risking that much of your own money. To get one, you pass an evaluation called a **challenge** run by a proprietary trading firm (prop firm).

## How a prop firm challenge works

1. **Pay an evaluation fee.** The fee depends on the account size.
2. **Hit the profit target** while following the rules — usually within one or two phases.
3. **Respect the loss limits.** Breaking a daily or maximum loss limit ends the challenge.
4. **Get funded.** Once you pass, you trade the funded account and receive a share of the profits.

## The rules you must know

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| Profit target | The gain you need, e.g. 10% |
| Daily loss limit | The most you can lose in one day, e.g. 4–5% |
| Maximum loss | The most the account can fall overall, e.g. 8–10% |
| Profit split | Your share of the profits, e.g. 80–90% |

## Tips to pass a challenge

- **Risk 0.5–1% per trade.** With a 4–5% daily limit, risking 2% per trade leaves very little room.
- **Avoid trading big news events** like NFP and CPI unless your plan is built for them.
- **Trade one or two markets you know well**, such as gold (XAUUSD) or EURUSD.
- **Stop for the day after 2 losses.** Protecting the daily limit is the key to passing.
- **Practise first** on a demo with the same rules.

## Funded account vs normal trading account

| | Funded account | Your own account |
|---|---|---|
| Money at risk | The challenge fee | Your whole deposit |
| Account size | Up to $200,000 | Whatever you deposit |
| Rules | Strict loss limits | Your own rules |
| Profits | 80–90% split | 100% yours |

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need experience to take a challenge?
It helps a lot. Most traders who pass have a tested strategy and strict risk rules.

### What happens if I fail?
You lose the evaluation fee, and you can buy a new challenge to try again.

### Are funded accounts real money?
Many prop firms use simulated accounts and pay you from their own profits. Read each firm's terms carefully.
